# Preprocessing, ROI strategies and shortcut-learning risks for ultrasound DL when brightness (echogenicity) is the label

Scope note: our task is ordinal renal cortical echogenicity Grade 0–3 relative to liver/spleen, using 975 B-mode JPGs (~180 patients), 2 machines (Toshiba, Canon Aplio 300), 4 image sizes, varying gain. Tags used below: **[US-kidney]** = kidney ultrasound evidence; **[US-liver]** = liver/HRI ultrasound evidence; **[US-other]** = other ultrasound; **[non-US]** = other modality; **[Inferred]** = my reasoning, not directly shown by a source. Search budget was limited (~15 tool calls), so many items are abstract-level; gaps are listed explicitly.

## Q1. Grayscale normalization across machines/gain: what preserves vs destroys echogenicity information

### Takeaway
No study found directly ablates histogram matching / z-score / CLAHE for an echogenicity-as-label task. The best-supported approach in the literature is an **internal reference-organ ratio** (hepatorenal-index style), because the label itself is defined relative to liver/spleen. But even HRI is documented as sensitive to gain, TGC, dynamic range and depth. Local, non-linear contrast methods (CLAHE) and per-image histogram equalization are most likely to destroy the signal and should be avoided on the model input (inferred).

### Cited Findings
- **[US-liver]** HRI (liver/kidney cortex brightness ratio) is used for steatosis grading; a DL + Gaussian-mixture pipeline automated HRI in 294 living-donor candidates. Agreement with radiologists was high for liver and kidney brightness individually, but only moderate-to-excellent for the HRI ratio itself. Manual HRI is described as more reproducible than visual grading but dependent on ROI selection — [Cha et al. 2021, PMC8446496](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8446496)
- **[US-liver]** Transfer-learned Inception-ResNet-v2 features + SVM reached AUC 0.977 for fatty liver vs 0.959 for HRI (biopsy reference) without needing ROI selection; Spearman correlation with steatosis level was slightly lower than HRI (0.78 vs 0.80) — [Byra et al. 2018, IJCARS](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11548-018-1843-2) (older than 5 years; included as a key baseline)
- **[US-liver]** HRI is described as a good indicator only "when it is measured properly using a well-defined grayscale imaging mode with optimal instrumental settings of gain, TGC, dynamic range, display map"; the filing suggests RF-based HRI is less sensitive to settings than B-mode HRI. (Patent, not peer-reviewed) — [USPTO 12551187](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12551187)
- **[US-liver]** HRI computation guidance: liver and kidney should be at similar imaging depth in the same plane; a near-horizontal interface leaves the kidney reference unreliable (patent) — [USPTO 12508000](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12508000)
- **[US-liver]** 2025 pilot in healthy volunteers: HRI/ATI reliability influenced by measurement depth, ROI size and subcutaneous fat; authors suggest standardized protocols and AI tools to improve reproducibility — [PMC12294838](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12294838/)
- **[US-kidney]** Analogue: renal resistive index reproducibility across three machines — machine type had a significant impact; variance correlated with kidney-transplant depth (summary from search; not HRI) — [search summary referencing Heliyon/related study](https://doaj.org/article/ee7906ebdaf949e3a684febc778f8c45)
- **[US-liver, lesions]** CLAHE evidence is weak and from different tasks: a thesis on focal liver lesions reported CNN accuracy 69.70% without vs 93.94% with CLAHE, but with overfitting, lesion-specific clip limits, and hemangioma not processed — [ITS repository](https://repository.its.ac.id/80450). A classical-ML liver-lesion study reported CLAHE improved sensitivity/lesion separability in some models — [Al-Quds repository](https://dspace.alquds.edu/items/ffc74e21-0db2-43e5-831b-ce8526f84b1f). Neither task uses absolute brightness as the label.
- **[US-kidney]** Pediatric renal DL study defined "hyperechogenicity" as cortex echogenicity above liver, poor cortico-medullary differentiation, or inverted echogenicity — i.e., the clinical label is inherently relative — [JMIR Med Inform 2022](https://medinform.jmir.org/2022/11/e40878/)
- **[US-liver]** Multi-view DL steatosis scoring (3,310 patients) reported high agreement across 3 premium scanners — [arXiv 2110.05664](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.05664); follow-up validation on an unseen scanner (Philips Affiniti 70) reported AUROC 0.891–0.936 across grades — [PMC12840080](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12840080/). Shows DL can transfer across scanners, but with far larger training data than ours.

### Inferences
- **[Inferred] Ratio-to-reference-organ (cortex/liver, cortex/spleen mean or median gray level) is the most label-aligned normalization.** Global gain on B-mode is approximately a monotonic remap of display intensities; a ratio cancels a purely multiplicative change but NOT log-compression/dynamic-range/gamma changes or depth-dependent TGC. So also log-differences (cortex − liver in dB-like gray units) and depth-matched ROIs are worth testing as features. Sampling liver ROI at the same depth as the cortex (per the HRI guidance above) should reduce TGC/depth bias.
- **[Inferred] CLAHE / per-image histogram equalization should NOT be applied to the classifier input** (they may be fine for the segmentation model): they are local/non-linear, equalize contrast between neighbouring tissues, and can compress exactly the cortex-vs-liver difference that defines Grade 1–3. If used at all, compute ratio features from the un-equalized image.
- **[Inferred] Per-image z-score** preserves within-image ordering and (being affine) preserves differences scaled by image SD, but the image SD depends on field-of-view content (amount of black background, sinus fat, text), so it can inject content-dependent bias. Prefer normalizing by statistics of a reference organ region (e.g., divide by liver/spleen median, or z-score using liver/spleen mask stats) — this is the "reference-organ normalization" version of z-score.
- **[Inferred] Histogram matching to a template** across machines is a monotonic remap and preserves ordering, but changes magnitudes of cortex/liver ratios non-uniformly; it is a reasonable baseline harmonizer for the CNN branch, but compute ratio features before matching, and validate that grade-wise ratio distributions are not distorted.
- **[Inferred] JPG screenshots** already have vendor post-processing, gamma/display map and compression baked in, so true acoustic normalization (RF-based, as patents suggest) is unavailable; recording gain/dynamic range from burned-in header text (before masking) as metadata covariates could be valuable.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found that directly compares histogram matching vs z-score vs CLAHE vs reference-organ ratio for a kidney echogenicity grading model, or for fatty-liver DL.
- No quantitative multi-vendor HRI variability under controlled gain/depth found (Heliyon 2024 TAI/TSI/HRI reproducibility study not read in full).

## Q2. ROI cropping vs whole image vs mask as extra channel vs attention guidance

### Takeaway
Evidence is mixed and organ-dependent: tight cropping sometimes helps (removes confounders) and sometimes hurts (removes context). For our task the "context" that matters is the reference organ, so a crop must contain **both kidney cortex and adjacent liver/spleen**. Mask-as-extra-channel is reported to help segmentation more than classification; mask-guided attention/regularization shows the largest reported gains in small datasets, including one hydronephrosis study.

### Cited Findings
- **[US-kidney]** Kuo et al. (4,505 kidney US images, eGFR labels, ImageNet ResNet): used kidney-length annotations to crop away the peripheral region; bootstrap aggregation against overfitting; Pearson r=0.741 AI-GFR vs creatinine-eGFR — [npj Digit Med 2019, PMC6550224](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6550224/) (no crop-vs-whole ablation reported in the snippet)
- **[US-kidney]** Two-stage pipeline: modified U-Net kidney segmentation then a dual-input classifier on original + segmented images; ResNet50 82.88% test accuracy for AKI-related echogenic enlargement — [ETASR](https://etasr.com/index.php/ETASR/article/download/16275/6301/81473) (lower-tier venue)
- **[US-other]** Achilles tendon US: cropping to the tendon via active contours gave **lower** accuracy than non-cropped images, suggesting surrounding tissue carries information — [arXiv 1909.04973](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.04973)
- **[US-other]** Breast lesion sonograms: lesion-ROI previously reported better than whole image; removing inner 66% of ROI did not significantly change performance (p=0.35), attributed to visible contour — [WSCG 2024](https://otik.zcu.cz/handle/11025/57396?locale=en)
- **[US-other]** Breast and liver tumor classification: tight cropping discards peritumoral information; tested crop expansion ratios 0.6–4.0 around tumor convex hull — [SPIE 2021](https://spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/11734/117340G/Towards-optimal-cropping--breast-and-liver-tumor-classification-using/10.1117/12.2589038.full)
- **[US-other]** Ovarian US preprint compares whole image vs lesion-guided rectangular ROI with margin vs expert contour vs masked radiomics (numbers not retrieved) — [arXiv 2608.25965](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25965)
- **[non-US / mixed]** SAMAug: stacking segmentation prior maps as extra image channels helped segmentation but "falls short" for classification; SAMAug-C was designed specifically for classification — [arXiv 2406.11026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11026)
- **[US-kidney, likely]** Rizhko et al. (MIDL 2024): segmentation masks used to guide model attention during training, "particularly effective in small dataset scenarios", for hydronephrosis diagnosis; in-distribution same or better (AUROC up to +0.28, AUPRC up to +0.33); on four external datasets AUROC rose from 0.02 to 0.75 and AUPRC 0.09–0.47 above baselines (abstract wording; modality inferred to be renal US, not confirmed from the page) — [PMLR v250](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v250/rizhko24a.html)
- **[non-US preprint]** Anatomy-guided attention regularization (Locus): loss term aligning encoder attention with a frozen segmentation-model mask — [arXiv 2607.10851](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.10851)
- **[US-liver]** Fatty-liver VGG19 trained on combined liver + kidney-liver ("CLKL") images reached 80.1% accuracy — [KNU research record](https://research.knu.ac.kr/en/publications/deep-learning-techniques-for-fatty-liver-using-multi-view-ultraso-2/), i.e., including the reference organ in the input is standard practice for relative echogenicity.

### Inferences
- **[Inferred] Recommended ranking for this project (to test, not proven):** (1) hand-crafted ratio features from masks (cortex/liver, cortex/spleen, sinus contrast, length) in a small model — strongest prior and least shortcut risk; (2) CNN on a crop around kidney ∪ adjacent liver/spleen boundary region with modest margin, outside-mask pixels optionally zeroed; (3) mask-guided attention loss as a regularizer; (4) mask as extra channel (weakest classification evidence). Whole-image input is the most shortcut-prone given burned-in text/calipers.
- **[Inferred]** Crops must be resized carefully: with 4 image sizes, resizing changes pixel spacing; kidney length in mm should be computed before resizing; texture/speckle size differences across machines may become a machine shortcut.
- **[Inferred]** A late-fusion design (CNN embedding + ratio features) lets the ablation show whether the CNN adds anything beyond the ratio.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head study found of crop vs mask-channel vs attention-guidance on the same ultrasound echogenicity task.
- Rizhko et al. full-text details (dataset size, mechanism, baselines, whether cropping was compared) not retrieved.

## Q3. Shortcut learning from calipers, text, annotations, machine artifacts; masking vs inpainting; detection

### Takeaway
Documented ultrasound evidence (fetal standard planes) shows models trained with embedded text/calipers lose ~11 accuracy points on clean images; removing the confounders during training recovers most of it, and **simple methods (interpolation, fast-marching inpainting) work as well as or better than deep inpainting; black boxes can hurt external performance**. Dermatology (ink markings) and chest X-ray (COVID) give the canonical cases; external validation alone does not prove absence of shortcuts.

### Cited Findings
- **[US-other, fetal]** Mikolaj et al. (DTU/Rigshospitalet): EfficientNet-B3 standard-plane classifier. Trained only on confounded images: 97.0% on confounded internal test vs 85.6% on clean internal test vs 80.5% external. All six removal methods (black box, noise fill, bilinear interpolation, fast marching [OpenCV], U-Net, GAN) raised clean-internal accuracy to ~91.7–93.8% (fast marching and U-Net 93.8%). With mixed training (77% confounded): clean internal 87.4% → up to 94.7% (U-Net); on the external set, black box (80.9%) and U-Net (81.3%) were significantly **lower** than baseline (84.7%). Authors: simple methods perform on par or better since they only replace corrupted regions; several methods leave visible artefacts that still carry spatial information about removed confounders; part of the gap attributed to clinicians annotating the best-quality images (label–annotation correlation) — [arXiv 2303.13918 (full text)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.13918)
- **[US-other]** Fan (sector) detection with U-Net then classical inpainting to remove annotations inside the fan — [arXiv 2102.02055](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.02055); HSV thresholding to detect coloured annotations and discard those pixels — [arXiv 2512.22730](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.22730); diffusion-based marker removal (Echo-DM) argues mask-based methods propagate mask errors and mask-free ones over-smooth texture — [emergentmind summary of arXiv 2606.09378](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2606.09378)
- **[US-other, thyroid]** Calipers carry nodule-location information: training segmentation on caliper-derived masks reached Dice 85.1% vs 90.4% with manual masks — [Buda et al., Duke Scholars](https://scholars.duke.edu/individual/pub1421611) (shows calipers are informative, i.e., a potential shortcut; not a shortcut study itself)
- **[non-US, dermatology]** Winkler et al. JAMA Dermatol 2019: gentian violet skin markings raised CNN melanoma scores for benign nevi (mean 0.16 → 0.54) and cut specificity 84.1% → 45.8%; cropping out markings restored specificity — [PMC6694463](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6694463); [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamadermatology/fullarticle/2740808)
- **[non-US, CXR]** DeGrave et al. Nat Mach Intell 2021: COVID-19 CXR models relied on confounders (data-source shortcuts) rather than pathology, found via explainable-AI methods; failed at new hospitals; external validation alone does not guarantee pathology-based reasoning; XAI recommended before deployment — [medRxiv preprint](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.09.13.20193565v2); [AHRQ PSNet summary](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/ai-radiographic-covid-19-detection-selects-shortcuts-over-signal)

### Inferences
- **[Inferred] Specific risks in our data:** (a) calipers/measurement text present mainly on certain images (e.g., when kidney length was measured, possibly more in abnormal/CKD kidneys) → label leakage; (b) burned-in gain/preset values in header text correlate with brightness; (c) machine identity (Toshiba vs Canon fonts, colour LUT, image size) may correlate with grade if referral patterns differ by machine; (d) image size itself (4 sizes) is a machine proxy.
- **[Inferred] Masking vs inpainting:** for a brightness-labelled task, inpainted pixels inside the cortex/liver ROIs would contaminate gray-level ratio features — **exclude caliper/text pixels from ROI statistics (mask them out of the organ masks) rather than relying on inpainted values**. For CNN input, use fast-marching/Telea or bilinear inpainting (per Mikolaj) rather than black boxes (black boxes lowered external accuracy and leave location cues). Also apply the same inpainting to random non-caliper locations in clean images ("sham inpainting") so the presence of an inpainted patch is not itself a cue.
- **[Inferred] Detection protocol:** (1) tabulate caliper/text presence, machine, image size, gain vs grade (chi-square) before training; (2) machine-stratified evaluation and leave-one-machine-out; (3) ablation: performance on images with vs without calipers, and with calipers synthetically added/removed (counterfactual test, like Winkler's marked vs unmarked); (4) Grad-CAM/occlusion saliency checking that attention falls on cortex and liver/spleen, not text/calipers/edges (DeGrave approach); (5) train a "machine classifier" probe on the grading model's embedding — high accuracy means machine information is retained; (6) patient-level splits (180 patients, ~5 images each) to avoid identity leakage.

### Gaps
- No found study specifically quantifying caliper shortcuts in breast/thyroid **classification** (only the thyroid caliper-as-weak-label study and the fetal study).
- No kidney-ultrasound shortcut study found.

## Q4. Domain shift between two machines: harmonization for tiny datasets

### Takeaway
Device shift in ultrasound DL is well documented. For tiny datasets, GAN/CycleGAN harmonization is unstable and risks altering brightness (the label); cheap options — reference-organ normalization, simple histogram matching as a baseline, intensity/gamma augmentation, machine-stratified validation, and fine-tuning — are the realistic choices, though direct ultrasound evidence is thin.

### Cited Findings
- **[US-other]** DCNNs trained on one ultrasound device do not perform well on data from a different device or acquisition protocol — [arXiv 1906.04301](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.04301v1.pdf)
- **[US-other]** Carotid US harmonization via image-to-image translation changed texture/noise while keeping anatomy; histogram correlation 0.920/0.844 vs 0.890/0.707 without adaptation — [arXiv 2103.12857](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.12857v3)
- **[US-other]** CycleGAN on unpaired ultrasound: low image quality and large inter-center variation make generator training unstable — [stability-enhanced CycleGAN 2022 (PDF)](https://www.cse.unr.edu/~bebis/CS791/Fall2025/ProjectIdeas/DomainShift_UDA/UsefulForProject/2022%20-%20A%20stability-enhanced%20CycleGAN%20for%20effective%20domain%20transformation%20of%20unpaired%20ultrasound%20images.pdf)
- **[non-US, MRI]** Review: harmonization and domain generalization both viable for small shifts — [Utrecht thesis review](https://studenttheses.uu.nl/handle/20.500.12932/42266); histogram matching used as a baseline that learned methods beat — [ISMRM 2023](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/23MProceedings/PDFfiles/1171_WizATtR63.html); argument that harmonization can degrade predictive accuracy and fine-tuning a source model may be better — [arXiv 2407.05163](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.05163v1)
- **[US-other]** Classical augmentation can produce unrealistic ultrasound images — [Lübeck cross-dataset augmentation study](https://research.uni-luebeck.de/en/publications/cross-data-set-generalization-of-ultrasound-image-augmentation-us/); breast US small-dataset augmentation analysis found certain augmentations far more effective than others — [Tupper & Gagné, arXiv 2403.09828](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.09828v1)
- **[US-liver]** Large-data DL steatosis model generalized to an unseen scanner (AUROC 0.891–0.936) — [PMC12840080](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12840080/)

### Inferences
- **[Inferred] Do not use GAN-based harmonization here**: with ~180 patients it is unstable, and any translation that alters gray levels directly alters the label (echogenicity). This is the strongest reason to avoid it.
- **[Inferred] Brightness/gamma augmentation is a double-edged sword**: global multiplicative brightness jitter applied identically to the whole image preserves cortex/liver ratios and teaches gain invariance (good); gamma/contrast jitter changes ratios non-linearly and could blur Grade 1 vs 2 boundaries — keep its range small and apply it to the whole image, never per-organ.
- **[Inferred] Practical stack:** reference-organ-normalized ratio features + machine as a covariate (or per-machine calibration of ratio thresholds, e.g., fit ordinal model with machine offset) + global brightness augmentation + leave-one-machine-out / machine-stratified CV reporting per-machine QWK/MAE; optional per-machine histogram matching for the CNN branch only, validated by checking that per-grade ratio distributions overlap across machines after normalization.

### Gaps
- No ultrasound-specific head-to-head of histogram matching vs augmentation vs fine-tuning for a two-scanner, ~1000-image classification problem found.
- Per-augmentation results of Tupper & Gagné not retrieved (unknown whether brightness/gamma were among the most effective).
