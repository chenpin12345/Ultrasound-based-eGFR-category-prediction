# Renal Cortical Echogenicity Grading (vs Liver/Spleen) and Sonographic Findings of CKD and AKI

Team working definition (for reference throughout): Grade 0 = cortex darker (hypoechoic) than reference organ; Grade 1 = similar (isoechoic); Grade 2 = brighter (hyperechoic); Grade 3 = markedly brighter, approaching renal sinus. Reference organ = liver for right kidney, spleen for left kidney.

Verification note: these notes are based on about 25 search and fetch calls. Several classic papers (Hricak 1982, Rosenfield 1981, Platt 1988, Manley & O'Neill 2001) could only be confirmed at the citation or abstract level, not from the full text. Each is flagged below.

---

## Q1. Original grading schemes and how they map to the team's Grade 0–3

### Takeaway
The commonly cited 4-level scheme (0 = less than liver, I = equal to liver, II = greater than liver, III = equal to renal sinus) is attributed to Hricak et al. 1982. It maps almost one-to-one onto the team's Grade 0–3. Many later papers collapse it to 3 levels (<, =, > reference), and liver is the default reference organ. No universally adopted standard exists, and the exact wording in the 1982 original could not be verified from full text.

### Cited Findings
- **Hricak H, et al. "Renal parenchymal disease: sonographic-histologic correlation." Radiology 1982;144(1):141–147.** DOI 10.1148/radiology.144.1.7089245. Retrospective study of 109 biopsied patients. Cortical echogenicity correlated positively with the severity of global sclerosis, focal tubular atrophy, hyaline casts per glomerulus and focal leukocytic infiltration. Renal length correlated with global sclerosis, tubular atrophy and casts. Echogenicity varied widely against BUN/creatinine. US could not distinguish *types* of medical renal disease. — [RSNA abstract](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/abs/10.1148/radiology.144.1.7089245); [Weill Cornell VIVO record](https://vivo.weill.cornell.edu/display/pubid7089245)
- **Grading attributed to Hricak (secondary source table):** Grade 0 = echogenicity lower than liver parenchyma (normal). Grade I = equal to liver (normal). Grade II = higher than liver (pathological). Grade III = equal to renal sinus (pathological). — reported in the table at [PMC3552768 Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3552768/table/tbl1) (secondary; not checked against the 1982 full text)
- **Rosenfield AT, Siegel NJ. "Renal parenchymal disease: histopathologic-sonographic correlation." AJR 1981;137:793–798.** Citation confirmed. Its grading or classification content could not be retrieved. — [citation in Springer reference list](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/BF02387855)
- **Platt JF, Rubin JM, Bowerman RA, Marn CS. "The inability to detect kidney disease on the basis of echogenicity." AJR 1988;151(2):317–319.** DOI 10.2214/ajr.151.2.317, PMID 3293376. The title signals a skeptical position, namely that echogenicity alone is an unreliable marker of disease. Full findings not verified. — [NCBI MedGen listing](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/medgen/1723255)
- **Manley JA, O'Neill WC. "How echogenic is echogenic? Quantitative acoustics of the renal cortex." Am J Kidney Dis 2001;37:706–711.** Quantitative mean pixel density (MPD) cortex/liver ratios in normal subjects, plus the hydration effect (as cited by Araújo 2020). Abstract not retrieved. — [cited in Araújo et al. BMC Nephrol 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7137523/); [citation in Thieme Ultraschall Med 2011](https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/html/10.1055/s-0029-1245992)
- **Moghazi S, et al. "Correlation of renal histopathology with sonographic findings." Kidney Int 2005;67(4):1515–1520.** DOI 10.1111/j.1523-1755.2005.00230.x. Retrospective study of 207 biopsied patients. Used a *quantitative* cortex/liver echogenicity ratio rather than a visual grade. Ratio >1.0 (cortex brighter than liver) was linked to severe chronic histologic disease in 66% vs 30% for ≤1.0. — [Stony Brook record](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/correlation-of-renal-histopathology-with-sonographic-findings/); [QxMD](https://read.qxmd.com/doi/10.1111/j.1523-1755.2005.00230.x)
- **Araújo NC, et al. BMC Nephrol 2020;21:123** (DOI 10.1186/s12882-020-01789-7). 3-level visual grade: cortex <, =, or > reference. **Liver for the right kidney; spleen for the left kidney, used when the liver was fatty (6 patients).** This is the same convention as the team's. — [PMC7137523](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7137523/)
- Pediatric nephrotic syndrome study (Iran), 3-level scheme against liver: grade 0 cortex < liver, grade 1 = liver, grade 2 > liver. Higher grade was associated with a more complicated course. — [KUMS PDF](https://www.kums.ac.ir/kums_content/media/image/2021/04/156002_orig.pdf)
- A Polish J Ultrason article describes grades 0–III comparing cortex, medulla and sinus echogenicity with liver or spleen parenchyma. — [jultrason.pl](https://jultrason.pl/artykul.php?a=510)
- A later review notes that the assumption "normal cortex is less reflective than liver/spleen" has been challenged: grade I (isoechoic) cortex can occur in normal kidneys. — [Egyptian paper via msjonline](https://www.msjonline.org/index.php/ijrms/article/download/1259/2606/11051) (as quoted in search snippet; PDF could not be parsed)

### Mapping table (team grade ↔ literature)
| Team grade | Hricak-type 4-level (as cited) | 3-level (<,=,>) schemes (Araújo 2020; Iranian pediatric) | Quantitative (Moghazi cortex/liver ratio) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 darker | Grade 0 (< liver), normal | "<" / grade 0 | ratio < 1.0 |
| 1 similar | Grade I (= liver), considered normal | "=" / grade 1 | ≈ 1.0 |
| 2 brighter | Grade II (> liver), pathological | ">" / grade 2 | > 1.0 |
| 3 markedly brighter | Grade III (= renal sinus), pathological | (merged into ">") | no published cut-off |

### Inferences
- The team's scheme is essentially the Hricak-type scheme. The difference is that the team's Grade 3 says "approaching" the sinus, while Hricak III says "equal to" the sinus. Annotators should be told which one applies.
- Grade 1 (isoechoic) is not reliably abnormal in adults. Most studies treat >reference as the abnormal threshold, which makes Grade ≥2 the natural binary "abnormal" cut.

### Gaps
- Full text of Hricak 1982 was not accessed, so the exact original wording of grades (and whether the 0–III scheme originates there or was later attributed to it) is unverified.
- The content of the Rosenfield 1981, Platt 1988 and Manley & O'Neill 2001 grading/quantitative definitions was not retrieved.
- No source was found defining a numeric ratio boundary between Grade 2 and Grade 3. **No consensus cut-offs map ratios to grades.**

## Q2. Evidence linking echogenicity to histopathology, creatinine, eGFR and CKD stage

### Takeaway
Cortical echogenicity is the US parameter that correlates *best* with chronic histologic damage. The correlations are still only weak to moderate (r ≈ 0.28–0.57), and echogenicity is specific but insensitive for CKD. Combining it with kidney length raises specificity.

### Cited Findings
- Moghazi 2005 (n=207): echogenicity had the strongest correlation of any US parameter with all four histologic parameters (r = 0.28–0.35). In multivariate analysis, tubular atrophy and interstitial inflammation were significant determinants of cortical echogenicity, but interstitial fibrosis was not. — [Stony Brook record](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/correlation-of-renal-histopathology-with-sonographic-findings/)
- Moghazi 2005 (as summarized in a 2022 review): increased echogenicity combined with decreased length is specific for severe CKD. Severe CKD can be characterized by combined (bilateral) kidney length <20 cm. — [Moses & Fernandez, POCUS J 2022, PMC9994307](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9994307/)
- Hricak 1982: echogenicity correlated with global sclerosis, tubular atrophy, hyaline casts and leukocytic infiltration. There was a wide variance against BUN/creatinine. — [RSNA](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/abs/10.1148/radiology.144.1.7089245)
- Libório et al., Kidney Blood Press Res 2017 (n=197, suspected glomerular disease): quantitative kidney/liver ratio. Best cut-off **>1.15** gave PPV 92% for eGFR <30 at 6 months, AUC 0.793. The ratio did *not* predict eGFR at biopsy, but correlated with follow-up eGFR (r = −0.684). Proposed for patients with kidney length >8 cm. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/da03193e070c494689d7fbbb8ed876df)
- Korean pediatric study (n=75 biopsies, 2017): echogenicity correlated with interstitial fibrosis and tubular atrophy but not with glomerular changes. — [Relationship of Renal Echogenicity with Renal Pathology and Function (BVS record)](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/gim/resource/ar/wpr-136749)
- Bangladeshi study (n=82): correlation with glomerular sclerosis r = 0.568, interstitial inflammation r = 0.493, interstitial fibrosis r = 0.365, tubular atrophy r = 0.394. Kidney length <9 cm plus cortical echogenicity ≥ liver predicted irreversible parenchymal disease with 93.3% sensitivity. — [Bangladesh J Radiol Imaging (banglajol)](https://banglajol.info/index.php/BJRI/article/view/90690)
- Nepali study: echogenicity grade correlated with serum creatinine r = 0.571 and eGFR r = −0.349. — [NHRC eLibrary record](https://elibrary.nhrc.gov.np/handle/20.500.14356/1306)
- Araújo 2020 (n=112): weighting kidney length/height by echogenicity grade (weights 1.17 / 1.00 / 0.69 for < / = / >) gave AUROC 0.838 for SCr >1.5 mg/dL and 0.812 for eGFR ≤45. For severe chronic histology (chronic score >7) AUROC was 0.845, with sensitivity 94.8% and specificity 40% at cut-off 0.43. No parameter detected acute glomerular changes. — [PMC7137523](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7137523/)
- Small study: cortex echogenicity for CKD had sensitivity 23%, specificity 96%, PPV 75%, NPV 73%. The authors favored intraparenchymal RI. — [CyberLeninka](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/features-of-dopplerographic-studies-in-the-diagnosis-of-chronic-kidney-disease)
- Thai glomerulonephritis cohort (computer-based image analysis): total renal echogenicity independently associated with progression (adjusted HR 1.13). — [GMSMJ](https://he02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/gmsmj/article/view/262871)
- **AI label definitions (Kuo et al., npj Digit Med 2019;2:29, DOI 10.1038/s41746-019-0104-2):** labels were **eGFR (abbreviated MDRD, from creatinine within 4 weeks of the scan)**, *not* echogenicity grades. CKD = eGFR <60. 4,505 images from 1,299 patients, GE scanners, single center in Taiwan. Images were cropped using the kidney-length caliper markers and fed to ResNet-101 with an XGBoost classifier. Results: r = 0.741, MAE 17.6. CKD classification accuracy 85.6%, AUC 0.904, sensitivity ~60.7%, specificity 92.1%. Four nephrologists scored 60.3–80.1% accuracy. — [PMC6550224](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6550224/)

### Inferences
- Echogenicity is a high-specificity, low-sensitivity sign. A normal (Grade 0–1) cortex does not exclude CKD.
- The correlation is stronger with tubulointerstitial/chronic changes than with glomerular or acute changes. Studies disagree on whether interstitial fibrosis itself drives echogenicity (Moghazi: no; Korean pediatric and Bangladeshi studies: yes).
- An ML project could use eGFR or CKD stage as labels, as Kuo did, or echogenicity grades as auxiliary labels. Grades are subjective (see Q5).

### Gaps
- No pooled sensitivity/specificity (meta-analysis) for visual echogenicity grade vs CKD stage was found.
- No study was found that reports per-grade (0/1/2/3) mean eGFR in a large cohort.

## Q3. Quantitative alternatives (HRI, cortex/liver ratio, spleno-renal ratio) and ROI technique

### Takeaway
Quantitative ratios use the mean gray level in a cortex ROI divided by (or into) a reference-organ ROI **at the same depth**. Both the nephrology literature (kidney/liver ratio; Moghazi, Libório) and the hepatology literature (hepatorenal index, HRI = liver/kidney for steatosis) use the method. The ratio direction differs between fields, so always state numerator and denominator.

### Cited Findings
- Libório 2017: ratio computed from mean pixel density of renal cortex and adjacent liver using ScionImage software. Cut-off >1.15 (see Q2). — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/da03193e070c494689d7fbbb8ed876df)
- Moghazi 2005: cortex/liver ratios in their cohort ranged about 0.71–1.89. Araújo used this range to derive grade weights. — [PMC7137523](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7137523/)
- HRI (hepatology) technique: HRI = mean liver echo-intensity / mean renal cortex echo-intensity, with ROIs placed **at the same depth**. Example ROI sizes: liver 200–250 mm² excluding vessels, bile ducts and focal lesions; renal cortex 20–30 mm² excluding large vessels, renal sinus, cysts and masses. Some protocols place ROIs near the image center. — [HRI technique summary, PMC8360521](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8360521/); [PMC9257875](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9257875)
- HRI pitfalls: operators may sample too little tissue or put the kidney ROI at a different depth. Values depend on kidney position: mid-pole 2.24 ± 0.20 vs upper pole 1.08 ± 0.16 in one study. Compute on original DICOM rather than PNG/JPEG. Use caution if liver fibrosis is stage ≥2. HRI cut-offs vs MRI-PDFF were 1.55 (grade 2) and 1.60 (grade 3 steatosis), with AUROC 0.83. — [Springer s44326-024-00005-2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44326-024-00005-2); [PMC7705486](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7705486)
- Spleen as reference (spleno-renal ratio): a preprint on diabetic nephropathy reported lower splenic/renal and hepatic/renal echointensity ratios in nephropathy. Urinary albumin correlated with the splenic/renal ratio (r = −0.58). **Not peer-reviewed.** — [Preprints.org 202306.0862](https://preprints.org/manuscript/202306.0862/v1)

### Inferences
- A practical recipe for the team: (1) use raw DICOM, (2) place the cortex ROI in the cortex lateral to the pyramids, avoiding sinus fat and arcuate vessels, (3) place the reference ROI in liver (RK) or spleen (LK) at the same depth, side by side on the same image, (4) record ratio = cortex/reference. Grade 2 roughly corresponds to ratio >1.0. This last mapping is an inference from Moghazi/Libório, not a validated standard.

### Gaps
- No validated spleno-renal ratio cut-off for CKD was found in peer-reviewed literature.
- **No consensus maps quantitative ratios to Grades 0–3.** 1.0 and 1.15 are single-study thresholds for "abnormal".

## Q4. Why spleen may be preferred, and confounders (fatty liver, cirrhosis, machine settings)

### Takeaway
Fatty liver and fibrosis raise liver brightness, which makes the kidney look relatively darker and can mask a true Grade 2. The spleen is less often affected, so it is used as a fallback or as the left-kidney reference. Gain, TGC, depth, focal zone, harmonics and vendor all change absolute gray levels, so only same-image, same-depth comparisons are meaningful.

### Cited Findings
- "One must have caution when making this comparison in patients with liver disease": fatty liver or fibrosis can raise liver echogenicity. — [Moses & Fernandez 2022, PMC9994307](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9994307/)
- Araújo 2020 switched to spleen and the left kidney in patients with fatty liver. — [PMC7137523](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7137523/)
- HRI rationale: fat increases hepatic backscatter and grayscale values. This is exactly the mechanism that biases a kidney-vs-liver grade toward lower grades. — [PMC8360521](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8360521/)
- Hydration and furosemide can alter cortical echogenicity (Manley & O'Neill hydration effect, as cited). Araújo considered the effect small. — [PMC7137523](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7137523/)
- Position/depth dependence of ratios (mid vs upper pole) and image-format effects. — [Springer s44326-024-00005-2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44326-024-00005-2)
- Kuo 2019 limited data to GE scanners from after 2014, an implicit acknowledgment of vendor effects. — [PMC6550224](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6550224/)

### Inferences
- Echogenicity is relative, so the reference organ must be normal. Flag images with steatosis, cirrhosis or splenomegaly/splenic disease, or exclude them.
- Splenomegaly or splenic disease could also distort the spleen reference. No primary study quantifying this was found.

### Gaps
- No primary studies were found that specifically quantify the effects of TGC, focal zone, harmonic imaging or vendor on cortex/liver or cortex/spleen ratios. These rest on general physics reasoning, not cited evidence.
- No direct head-to-head comparison of liver vs spleen as reference for CKD grading was found.

## Q5. Inter- and intraobserver agreement

### Takeaway
**No study reporting kappa values specifically for visual renal cortical echogenicity grading was found.** Agreement data exist for renal measurements (length, cortical thickness) and for other ordinal US grading systems, and these can serve as methodological templates.

### Cited Findings
- Yamashita SR, et al., Radiol Bras 2015;48(1):12–16 (n=54 CKD, two blinded radiologists). Interobserver agreement was excellent for cortical thickness (0.754) and bipolar length (0.833) and satisfactory for parenchymal thickness (0.523). — [SciELO](https://www.scielo.br/j/rb/a/VLcHYqjHh8Pv9PWbbrJYXDH/?lang=en)
- Beland et al., AJR 2010: cortical thickness has excellent interobserver agreement and correlates with eGFR better than length (n=25). — cited in [PMC9994307](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9994307/)
- Template for ordinal grading reliability: SFU fetal hydronephrosis grading κ 0.620–0.825, Onen κ 0.618–0.724 (Landis–Koch bands). — [Obstet Gynecol Sci 2019 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/fcd93d98b6514fe1a5b2221ae8da7950)
- Qualitative cortical echogenicity evaluation in humans is reported (in a veterinary paper's literature review) to have high inter- and intra-observer variation. — [BMC Vet Res 2015](https://bmcvetres.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s12917-015-0415-8)
- Araújo 2020 used a single operator and did not report agreement. — [PMC7137523](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7137523/)

### Inferences
- The team should measure its own weighted kappa (linear or quadratic) for the 0–3 scale, both between annotators and within each annotator (re-reading). This would be a novel contribution given the literature gap.

### Gaps
- No kappa values for echogenicity grading were found, and no reproducibility data were found for quantitative kidney/liver ratios in a CKD setting.

## Q6. Sonographic signs of CKD and AKI (with thresholds)

### Takeaway
CKD shows small kidneys, thin cortex, increased echogenicity, loss of corticomedullary differentiation (CMD) and lobulated/irregular contour. AKI kidneys are usually normal or enlarged, sometimes echogenic. In AKI, ultrasound's main job is to exclude obstruction, which is uncommon.

### Cited Findings
**Normal and CKD**
- Normal adult length: median 11.2 cm (left) and 10.9 cm (right) in 665 volunteers. Length correlates with height (Emamian, AJR 1993). — [PMC9994307](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9994307/)
- O'Neill (Kidney Week 2012 slides): normal length 10–12 cm, and no data exist on how to correct for body size. — [AJKD blog](https://ajkdblog.org/2012/11/02/kidney-week-2012-radiology-of-the-kidney/)
- Cortical thickness is measured from the base of the medullary pyramid to the kidney edge, and is normally about 7–10 mm (O'Neill CJASN 2014, DOI 10.2215/CJN.03170313). When pyramids are not visible, parenchymal thickness ~1.5–2.0 cm is used instead. Accentuated lobulation suggests cortical thinning. — [PMC pmid 24458082](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/pmid/24458082). The 2012 slides give 5–9 mm, which **conflicts** with this. — [AJKD blog](https://ajkdblog.org/2012/11/02/kidney-week-2012-radiology-of-the-kidney/)
- Normal cortical thickness 8–10 mm, decreasing stepwise with function (Takata 2016; Beland 2010). — [PMC9994307](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9994307/)
- Severe CKD: combined bilateral length <20 cm (Moghazi). Increased echogenicity plus reduced length is specific for severe CKD. — [PMC9994307](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9994307/)
- Length <9 cm plus echogenicity ≥ liver predicted irreversible disease (sens 93.3%). — [banglajol](https://banglajol.info/index.php/BJRI/article/view/90690)
- Height-adjusted cortical thickness 4.0 mm/cm predicted a 30% decline or dialysis (sens 72.5%, spec 80%). — [J Clin Med 2018, 10.3390/jcm7120527](https://dx.doi.org/10.3390/jcm7120527)
- In renal parenchymal disease, cortical echogenicity increases and CMD becomes indistinct (WFUMB course chapter). — [arts.units.it Chapter 17](https://arts.units.it/bitstream/11368/2991851/2/Chapter_17.pdf)
- Radiopaedia: increased renal echogenicity is non-specific. Causes include normal variation, CKD, amyloidosis, sickle cell disease and HIV nephropathy. — [Radiopaedia: Increased renal echogenicity](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/31795)

**AKI**
- Podoll et al., BMC Nephrol 2013 (n=810 AKI inpatients): US was normal in 62% and hydronephrosis was found in 5%. Obstruction was the cause in ~2.3%. US is most useful when history suggests obstruction. — [BMC Nephrol 14:188](https://bmcnephrol.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1471-2369-14-188)
- Hydronephrosis in <10% of AKI evaluations. Non-dilated obstruction can occur. — [PMC9994307](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9994307/)
- ATN: enlarged kidney, reduced respiratory mobility, thickened capsule. Early ATN raises cortical echogenicity while sparing the pyramids, so CMD is preserved. Severe ATN makes the pyramids echogenic and reduces CMD. An increased AP/longitudinal ratio indicates worse prognosis. Acute interstitial nephritis increases echogenicity. RI normal is 0.5–0.7, and elevated RI is associated with persistent AKI and ICU mortality. — [PMC9994307](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9994307/)
- Enlarged kidneys in AKI suggest infiltrative disease (lymphoma, monoclonal gammopathy), proliferative GN, ATN, AIN or renal vein thrombosis. Enlarged echogenic kidneys with lost CMD in AKI contrast with small, thin-cortex CKD kidneys. — [PMC2891573](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2891573)
- AKI kidneys were significantly larger than controls (Liu & Wang, Transl Androl Urol 2020). — [TAU](https://tau.amegroups.org/article/view/45403/html)

### Inferences
- Grade ≥2 echogenicity is *not* specific for CKD, because AKI (ATN, AIN, GN) can also raise it. Size and cortical thickness separate the two: small and thin points to CKD, while normal or large points to AKI or acute-on-chronic disease.

### Gaps
- Several thresholds conflict: normal cortical thickness 5–9 vs 7–10 vs 8–10 mm, and "small kidney" <9 cm vs <10 cm vs combined <20 cm. There is **no consensus** threshold.

## Q7. Pediatric differences

### Takeaway
In neonates and young infants the renal cortex is normally isoechoic or hyperechoic relative to liver, especially in premature and low-birth-weight infants. Adult grading rules (Grade ≥1–2 = abnormal) must not be applied to them. Transient increased echogenicity also occurs in acutely ill children.

### Cited Findings
- Hricak H, et al. "Neonatal kidneys: sonographic-anatomic correlation." Radiology 1983;147(3):699–702. Neonatal cortex echogenicity equals liver, attributed to glomeruli occupying a larger proportion of the cortex in the first 2 months. — [RSNA](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/radiology.147.3.6844606)
- In 49 healthy newborns (1–27 days), the cortex was isoechoic or more echogenic than liver in most premature and small term newborns. Newborns over 3000 g were more often hypoechoic. — [Acta Medica Medianae 2000](https://publisher.medfak.ni.ac.rs/AMM_1/amm-stari/2000-html/1-broj/Predrag%20Miljkovic-abs.htm)
- Increased parenchymal echogenicity in acutely ill children can be transient and does not necessarily mean renal disease (AJR 2008, cited by Radiopaedia). — [Radiopaedia](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/31795)
- Pediatric nephrotic syndrome: a higher grade vs liver was associated with a complicated course. — [KUMS PDF](https://www.kums.ac.ir/kums_content/media/image/2021/04/156002_orig.pdf)
- Pediatric biopsy (Korea): echogenicity correlated with IF/TA, not glomerular change. — [BVS record](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/gim/resource/ar/wpr-136749)

### Inferences
- In a dataset, flag or exclude patients under about 6 months (age cut-off not firmly established) or use age-specific labels.

### Gaps
- The exact age at which the cortex becomes hypoechoic to liver is not established. One aggregator claims ~6 months but is untraceable, so it was not used as a source.
